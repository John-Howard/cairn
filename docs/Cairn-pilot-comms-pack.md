# Cairn — Pilot Communications Pack

**Status:** v0.2. Supports pilot stage **P3** (*Intake & Pilot Plan* §5): communications for the first two functions — **Prevention & Community Safety** and **HR** — then reusable, department by department, for the full information audit (P6). Covers **both intake question sets**: the *activity* set (what the team does with information about people) and the *asset* set (the systems, files and records the team holds it in).
**Tone rules (Question Set §3):** conversational, never "audit of you"; jargon-free — say *personal information*, not *personal data processing* or *Article 30*; say *systems, files and records*, not *information assets*, until the term has been introduced; "don't know" is explicitly welcome; shadow data (spreadsheets, inboxes, paper) is an amnesty, not a confession.
**Placeholders:** `[SPONSOR]` chief/deputy chief fire officer or SIRO · `[IG LEAD]` name + contact · `[FUNCTION]` department · `[LINK]` the activity-intake URL · `[ASSET LINK]` the asset-intake URL · `[DATE]` deadline · `[SESSION]` drop-in details.

Send order: 1 → 4 (with 5 attached or linked) → 2 and/or 3 → 6 as needed → 7 on completion. Use **2** for activity intake and **3** for asset intake; asset intake is invited function-by-function alongside the audit waves (`Cairn-intake-pilot-plan.md` §5), so a function doing both in one wave receives both — send them together, to the same person or to different ones as the manager has nominated (see 4). Items 1, 4, 5, 6 and 7 serve both sets; the bracketed alternatives in each mark the parts to keep or drop.

---

## 1. Senior sponsor announcement

*From `[SPONSOR]`, to the pilot functions (all-staff at P6). Short deliberately — its job is mandate and tone, not detail.*

> **Subject: Helping us protect the information we hold about people**
>
> Every day our service holds information about the people we protect — householders we visit, people involved in incidents, our own staff. We have a legal duty to keep a clear record of what information we hold, why, and how we look after it. More importantly, it's how we make sure the trust people place in us is deserved.
>
> Over the coming weeks, `[FUNCTION]` will help us build that record. There are two halves to it, and we need both: **what your teams do** with information about people, and **where that information lives** — the systems, shared drives, filing cabinets and log books you rely on. `[IG LEAD]` will ask a nominated person in each team to spend around an hour on plain-English questions about one of these, or a little longer where we ask for both — no data protection or IT knowledge needed, and "don't know" is a perfectly good answer.
>
> This is not an inspection of any team or person. Nobody is checking up on you; we're recording what we do and what we hold so we can protect it properly. Please give it your support and your honesty — especially about the informal stuff: the spreadsheets, the paper forms, the inbox folders. Those are exactly what we most need to know about, and no one will be criticised for surfacing them.
>
> Thank you — this matters, and it protects all of us.
>
> `[SPONSOR]`

## 3. Asset-intake respondent invitation

> **Subject: Your part in the information asset register — about 15 minutes per item, by `[DATE]`**
>
> Hello `[NAME]`,
>
> Your manager has nominated you to help us record the information assets `[TEAM]` looks after — thank you for taking it on.
>
> **What you'll do.** Sign in at `[ASSET LINK]` with your normal work account and answer a set of plain-English questions about each *information asset* your team looks after — a system, a database, an application, or a collection of paper or physical records, e.g. "the HR system" or "the station log books". One run of the questions per asset; most teams have a handful. You'll be asked about things you already know: what it is, who looks after it day to day and who the senior person responsible for it is, whether it holds information about people, where it's kept and whether an outside company hosts it, how it's protected, and how long things are kept. Each run takes around 15 minutes, and you can save and come back at any point.
>
> **What you don't need.** Any IT or data protection expertise. The questions are about what you already know, not technical detail — and we don't ask you to judge how sensitive the information is or what the legal position on it is. Those are our job. If you don't know an answer, tick **"Don't know — ask the IG team to follow up"** and move on: an honest "don't know" is genuinely more useful to us than a guess.
>
> **Three things to know:**
> - *This covers systems and paper alike, and everything counts whether or not it holds information about people* — a shared drive, a filing cabinet, a spreadsheet and a supplier-hosted system are all information assets. If your team relies on it to hold information, list it.
> - *Nothing you enter is published anywhere.* Your answers create a draft register entry that the information governance team reviews before anything is finalised.
> - *There's no blame for informal arrangements.* If something is kept in a personal drive, an inbox folder, or a cupboard rather than a proper system, tell us anyway — that's exactly what we need to know about.
>
> **Support.** Drop-in session `[SESSION]`, or contact me any time: `[IG LEAD CONTACT]`. Afterwards I'll book a short conversation to finish off the entries — anything you marked "don't know", plus a few things only we can complete, like matching the senior person you named to their account. If I come back to you about one of those, it doesn't mean you got it wrong.
>
> `[If also invited for activity intake:` *You'll have had a second email about the work `[TEAM]` does with information about people. That's the other half of the same exercise — do them in either order.*`]`
>

## 4. Manager briefing note

*From `[IG LEAD]` to the head of each pilot function, before anything reaches respondents.*

> **Subject: Information audit — what `[FUNCTION]` is being asked to do**
>
> `[FUNCTION]` is one of the first two areas piloting our information audit (with `[OTHER FUNCTION]`). Here's what it involves and what I need from you.
>
> **The two halves.** We're recording two things, and they're separate sets of questions:
> - **What your teams do** with information about people — "carrying out home fire safety visits", "recruiting new staff". One run per activity; most teams have two to five.
> - **Where that information lives** — the systems, shared drives, paper files and log books your teams rely on. One run per item; most teams have a handful.
>
> `[FUNCTION]` is being asked for `[both / activities only / assets only]` in this wave.
>
> **The ask.** Please nominate **one respondent per team for each half** — the person who actually knows the day-to-day reality, not necessarily the most senior. It can be the same person for both, but often isn't: the person who knows the work isn't always the person who looks after the system. I need names by `[DATE-1]`. Time commitment is roughly **an hour to 90 minutes** for the activity questions and **about 15 minutes per system or record set** for the asset questions, plus a short follow-up conversation with my team.
>
> **What they'll do.** Answer plain-English questions in Cairn (our new records system) — no data protection or IT expertise needed, and "don't know" is a valid answer throughout. Your area was chosen for the pilot deliberately `[Prevention: because your work involves some of our most sensitive information — vulnerable people, children, health` / `HR: because your processing is high-volume and well understood]`, which makes your feedback especially valuable.
>
> **One thing that lands on you personally.** The asset questions ask who the *senior person responsible* for each system or record set is — the person who would decide if it changed or was got rid of. For `[FUNCTION]`'s systems that will often be you. I'll confirm each of those with you before it's recorded; it's a named accountability, not an administrative label, and it's the basis of how we'll ask you to review the entry periodically.
>
> **What I need from you beyond names:** signal clearly to your teams that this has your backing and is safe to be honest in — especially about informal records. If a team keeps its own spreadsheet, or the real working copy lives in someone's inbox, that's precisely what we need to hear about, with no comeback.
>
> **What you get back:** a verified record of your area's information handling and of what you hold it in — which in practice means faster answers when things go wrong, evidence when you need to share data with partners, a clear line on who owns what, and no more scrambling when someone asks "what do we hold, where, and why?".
>
> `[IG LEAD]`

## 5. Respondent FAQ *(attach to 2 and 3, or publish on the intranet)*

> **Is this an audit of me or my team?**
> No. Nobody's performance is being assessed. We're building a record of what the service does with personal information — a legal requirement, and the foundation for protecting it properly. Honesty helps you; it can't hurt you.
>
> **Why me?**
> Because you know the actual work. The record is only worth having if it matches reality, and the people who know reality are the ones doing the job.
>
> **What if I don't know an answer?**
> Tick "Don't know" and move on. It flags the question for the IG team to pick up with you later — that's the system working as designed, not a failure.
>
> **What counts as "personal information"?**
> Anything that could identify a living person: names, addresses, phone numbers, photos, case references, vehicle registrations, even a note about someone. Information about businesses counts too where it points at a person (a named duty holder, a sole trader).
>
> **I've been sent two sets of questions. What's the difference?**
> One asks about **what your team does** with information about people (an *activity* — "carrying out home fire safety visits"). The other asks about **where information lives** (an *information asset* — "the HR system", "the station log books"). Some people are asked for one, some for both. They're separate runs and can be done in either order.
>
> **What counts as an "information asset"?**
> Anything your team relies on to hold information: a computer system or online service, a database, an application, paper files, or equipment and media that hold information. A shared drive, a filing cabinet, a supplier-hosted system and a long-running spreadsheet all count.
>
> **Does an asset still count if it holds nothing about people?**
> Yes. We're recording everything the service holds information in, not only the parts covered by data protection law. One of the questions asks whether it holds information about people — "no" is a perfectly good answer and doesn't take it off the register.
>
> **We keep our own spreadsheet / paper file / inbox folder. Should I mention it?**
> **Yes — please.** These informal records are the most important thing the audit can surface, and there is no comeback for declaring them. The risk is in *not* knowing they exist. Mention it in both sets of questions if it's relevant to both.
>
> **Do I need to know the technical details of a system, or how sensitive its information is?**
> No. We don't ask you to classify anything or to judge the legal position — the IG team sets those when we review your answers. The asset questions only ask what you already know from using the thing.
>
> **The asset questions ask who's "senior person responsible". What if I'm not sure?**
> Give your best answer or tick "don't know". We check every one of these against an actual person and confirm it with them, so you're pointing us in a direction, not making the appointment. Being asked about it afterwards isn't a sign you got it wrong.
>
> **What happens to my answers?**
> They create *draft* records visible only to the information governance team, who will review them with you, fill the gaps, and only then put them forward for approval — formal sign-off by the Data Protection Officer for activities, and by the IG team for register entries. Nothing is published or reported from drafts.
>
> **How long will it take?**
> Activities: 15–20 minutes each, and most respondents have two to five. Assets: around 15 minutes each, and most teams have a handful. You can save and come back whenever you like.
>
> **What happens afterwards?**
> A short follow-up conversation — about anything you marked "don't know", and about the few things only the IG team can finish, like matching a named owner to their account or a system name to a supplier we already have on record. Then we do the technical parts (legal bases, retention rules, how sensitive the information is). You'll be asked to confirm the finished record matches what your team actually does and actually uses — the only test that matters.

## Usage notes (IG team)

- **Pre-fill before inviting** (Question Set §3): create draft activities for the processing you already know about, and bulk-load the assets you already have a list of via the asset CSV import, so respondents correct rather than create. The invitations' "two to five activities" and "a handful" claims should be true for that team — check before sending.
- **Check both question sets are seeded** before a wave that uses both (`Cairn-pilot-p1-seeding-checklist.md` §D), and put the right URL in each invitation — activity intake and asset intake are different screens (`[LINK]` vs `[ASSET LINK]`).
- **Timing:** sponsor announcement ~1 week before invitations; manager note in between; `[DATE]` two to three weeks after invitations land. Drop-in in week one. Where a function is doing both sets in one wave, send 2 and 3 together rather than staggering them — two emails a fortnight apart read as two separate exercises.
- **Asset intake is invited function-by-function, alongside the audit waves** (decided, `Cairn-intake-pilot-plan.md` §5) — not opened to all contributors at once. Send 3 to a function's nominated asset respondent(s) on the same cadence as 2, whether that's the same wave as its activity audit or a later one.
- **Never ask respondents to classify.** Security classification and s62 logging scope are curator judgements, deliberately absent from the asset questions (IAR Question Set §1) — don't reintroduce them in a covering email or a drop-in.
- **Set expectations about follow-ups on asset intake:** unlike activity intake, a gap there is not always a "don't know" — the named senior owner always raises one, and unmatched department, supplier or retention names raise one too (IAR Question Set §1). Items 3, 5 and 7 say so, so a respondent contacted about an answered question doesn't read it as a correction. Keep that wording if you edit them.
- **At P6 (full rollout)**, reuse this pack wave by wave with the P5 retrospective's wording fixes applied — and update the pilot-specific lines (e.g. "one of the first two areas").
- The same pack, lightly edited, becomes the **periodic review** communications when either question set is reused at review cycles (Plan §5) — for assets, that review is addressed to the named owner recorded at curation, so item 4's owner paragraph is the one to lead with.
