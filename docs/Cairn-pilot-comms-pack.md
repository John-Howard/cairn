# Cairn — Pilot Communications Pack

**Status:** v0.1. Supports pilot stage **P3** (*Intake & Pilot Plan* §5): communications for the first two functions — **Prevention & Community Safety** and **HR** — then reusable, department by department, for the full information audit (P6).
**Tone rules (Question Set §3):** conversational, never "audit of you"; jargon-free — say *personal information*, not *personal data processing* or *Article 30*; "don't know" is explicitly welcome; shadow data (spreadsheets, inboxes, paper) is an amnesty, not a confession.
**Placeholders:** `[SPONSOR]` chief/deputy chief fire officer or SIRO · `[IG LEAD]` name + contact · `[FUNCTION]` department · `[LINK]` the Cairn intake URL · `[DATE]` deadline · `[SESSION]` drop-in details.

Send order: 1 → 2 → 3 (with 4 attached or linked) → 5 as needed → 6 on completion.

---

## 1. Senior sponsor announcement

*From `[SPONSOR]`, to the pilot functions (all-staff at P6). Short deliberately — its job is mandate and tone, not detail.*

> **Subject: Helping us protect the information we hold about people**
>
> Every day our service holds information about the people we protect — householders we visit, people involved in incidents, our own staff. We have a legal duty to keep a clear record of what information we hold, why, and how we look after it. More importantly, it's how we make sure the trust people place in us is deserved.
>
> Over the coming weeks, `[FUNCTION]` will help us build that record. `[IG LEAD]` will ask a nominated person in each team to spend around an hour answering plain-English questions about the work you do — no data protection knowledge needed, and "don't know" is a perfectly good answer.
>
> This is not an inspection of any team or person. Nobody is checking up on you; we're recording what we do so we can protect it properly. Please give it your support and your honesty — especially about the informal stuff: the spreadsheets, the paper forms, the inbox folders. Those are exactly what we most need to know about, and no one will be criticised for surfacing them.
>
> Thank you — this matters, and it protects all of us.
>
> `[SPONSOR]`

## 2. Respondent invitation

*From `[IG LEAD]` to each nominated respondent, after their manager has confirmed the nomination (see 3).*

> **Subject: Your part in the information audit — about an hour, by `[DATE]`**
>
> Hello `[NAME]`,
>
> Your manager has nominated you to record what `[TEAM]` does with information about people — thank you for taking it on.
>
> **What you'll do.** Sign in at `[LINK]` with your normal work account and answer a set of plain-English questions about each *activity* your team does that involves information about people — things like "carrying out home fire safety visits" or "recruiting new staff". One run of the questions per activity; most people have between two and five. Each takes 15–20 minutes, and you can save and come back at any point.
>
> **What you don't need.** Any data protection knowledge. The questions are about your day job, not the law. If you don't know an answer, tick **"Don't know — ask the IG team to follow up"** and move on: an honest "don't know" is genuinely more useful to us than a good guess.
>
> **Three things to know:**
> - *"Personal information" means anything that could identify a living person* — a name, an address, a photo, a case reference, even a note about someone. If in doubt, include it.
> - *Nothing you enter is published anywhere.* Your answers create draft records that the information governance team reviews with you before anything is finalised.
> - *Please tell us about the informal stuff* — spreadsheets, shared drives, paper files, notebooks. This is the most valuable part of the whole exercise and carries no blame whatsoever.
>
> **Support.** Drop-in session `[SESSION]`, or contact me any time: `[IG LEAD CONTACT]`. Afterwards I'll book a short conversation to go through anything marked "don't know".
>
> Please aim to finish by **`[DATE]`**. Thank you — this genuinely matters.
>
> `[IG LEAD]`

## 3. Manager briefing note

*From `[IG LEAD]` to the head of each pilot function, before anything reaches respondents.*

> **Subject: Information audit — what `[FUNCTION]` is being asked to do**
>
> `[FUNCTION]` is one of the first two areas piloting our information audit (with `[OTHER FUNCTION]`). Here's what it involves and what I need from you.
>
> **The ask.** Please nominate **one respondent per team** — the person who actually knows the day-to-day work, not necessarily the most senior. I need names by `[DATE-1]`. Their time commitment is roughly **an hour to 90 minutes** in total, plus a short follow-up conversation with my team.
>
> **What they'll do.** Answer plain-English questions in Cairn (our new records system) about each activity involving information about people. Your area was chosen for the pilot deliberately `[Prevention: because your work involves some of our most sensitive information — vulnerable people, children, health` / `HR: because your processing is high-volume and well understood]`, which makes your feedback especially valuable.
>
> **What I need from you beyond names:** signal clearly to your teams that this has your backing and is safe to be honest in — especially about informal records. If a team keeps its own spreadsheet, that's precisely what we need to hear about, with no comeback.
>
> **What you get back:** a verified record of your area's information handling — which in practice means faster answers when things go wrong, evidence when you need to share data with partners, and no more scrambling when someone asks "what do we hold and why?".
>
> `[IG LEAD]`

## 4. Respondent FAQ *(attach to 2, or publish on the intranet)*

> **Is this an audit of me or my team?**
> No. Nobody's performance is being assessed. We're building a record of what the service does with personal information — a legal requirement, and the foundation for protecting it properly. Honesty helps you; it can't hurt you.
>
> **Why me?**
> Because you know the actual work. The record is only worth having if it matches reality, and the people who know reality are the ones doing the job.
>
> **What if I don't know an answer?**
> Tick "Don't know" and move on. It flags the question for the IG team to pick up with you later — that's the system working as designed, not a failure.
>
> **What counts as "personal information"?**
> Anything that could identify a living person: names, addresses, phone numbers, photos, case references, vehicle registrations, even a note about someone. Information about businesses counts too where it points at a person (a named duty holder, a sole trader).
>
> **We keep our own spreadsheet / paper file / inbox folder. Should I mention it?**
> **Yes — please.** These informal records are the most important thing the audit can surface, and there is no comeback for declaring them. The risk is in *not* knowing they exist.
>
> **What happens to my answers?**
> They create *draft* records visible only to the information governance team, who will review them with you, fill the gaps, and only then put them forward for formal sign-off by the Data Protection Officer. Nothing is published or reported from drafts.
>
> **How long will it take?**
> 15–20 minutes per activity, and most respondents have two to five activities. You can save and come back whenever you like.
>
> **What happens afterwards?**
> A short follow-up conversation about anything marked "don't know", then the IG team does the technical parts (legal bases, retention rules). You'll be asked to confirm the finished record matches what your team actually does — the only test that matters.
